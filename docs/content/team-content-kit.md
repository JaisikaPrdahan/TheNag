## Article template — [Person 1: Ingestion + extraction]

**Title:** Why a job opportunity should start as evidence, not an alert

Every day, job seekers save opportunities they mean to revisit. The problem is not a lack of listings; it is the lack of context when the moment to act arrives. TheNag turns a caption or video into structured evidence: role, company, location, skills, deadline, source, and confidence.

That evidence is useful only when uncertainty is visible. A missing deadline stays missing. A weakly extracted detail is not dressed up as fact. The result is a calmer starting point for a real decision.

## LinkedIn template — [Person 1: Ingestion + extraction]

Job discovery does not need another noisy alert stream. It needs a way to preserve the details that make an opportunity worth revisiting. At TheNag, we begin with evidence from captions and uploaded videos, then keep uncertainty visible. Better inputs make better decisions.

## Article template — [Person 2: Classification + confidence]

**Title:** Confidence is more useful than certainty theatre

Opportunity content is often incomplete, translated, or stale. TheNag classifies listings into Jobs & Gigs, Interviews & Hiring Drives, or Uncertain, then combines extraction quality with the source’s observed reliability. A score is never a promise; it is a concise explanation of what the system knows and what it does not.

## LinkedIn template — [Person 2: Classification + confidence]

“High confidence” should mean something. TheNag separates extraction quality from source history, then explains the combined result directly on each opportunity. Clear uncertainty is a feature, not a failure.

## Article template — [Person 3: Memory + trust]

**Title:** A job assistant should remember taste, not just clicks

Saving a role, rejecting one, or repeatedly skipping a format are all signals. TheNag uses them as private per-user memory to reduce repetition and improve ranking over time. It also keeps a separate shared source-trust bank so one creator’s history can improve confidence without ever exposing personal behavior.

The boundary matters. Your preferences are not a product feed. Emails and phone numbers are redacted before memory retention.

## LinkedIn template — [Person 3: Memory + trust]

The strongest recommendation is explainable: “You saved similar React roles, prefer remote work, and this source has a reliable history.” TheNag makes those signals visible—and keeps personal memory private.

## Article template — [Person 4: Frontend]

**Title:** The “Why this?” button is part of the product

Recommendations are easier to trust when they can be inspected. TheNag gives every opportunity a visible Why panel: recalled preferences, duplicate state, changed details, source evidence, and confidence. It turns a ranking into a conversation the job seeker can challenge.

## LinkedIn template — [Person 4: Frontend]

We designed TheNag around a simple belief: a recommendation without an explanation is just another alert. Every card includes “Why this?” so the user can see the preference, change, and source signals behind it.
